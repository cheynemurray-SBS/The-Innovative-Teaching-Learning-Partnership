Partner with the Instructional Technology team to bring new technology-enhanced learning experiences into your courses. What a Partnership Can Accomplish

A few examples of the kind of work this partnership is built to support:

  • Build immersive VR/AR learning modules in the XR Lab for experiential or simulation-based learning
  • Prototype physical models, manipulatives, or student projects using the Makerspace
  • Produce short animated or narrated videos to engage students in asynchronous lectures
  • Design and build interactive Canvas modules for a fully redesigned course
  • Develop AI-supported assessments and adaptive learning activities
  • Manage and standardize multiple sections of a Canvas course
  • Design a computer lab–based workshop or hands-on lab experience tied to course outcomes

XR Lab

For example

The XR Lab is dedicated to immersive, experiential learning through Virtual Reality. Your discipline may already have a VR application — a virtual anatomy lab, a historical walkthrough, a chemistry simulation — that just needs to be licensed. Fund the software so your course gets hands-on VR time in the lab.

Makerspace

For example

Students are asked to design a solution on paper but never build it. Give them a Makerspace project where they prototype, test, and iterate on a physical model as part of the course.

Canvas

For example

A course still runs on static PDFs and slide decks. Rebuild a unit as an interactive Canvas module with embedded checks for understanding and a clear mastery path.

Department-Oriented Projects

For example

Fund a piece of specialized equipment a specific department needs but couldn't otherwise budget for — like an augmented reality topographic sandbox for Environmental Studies (EVST), giving students hands-on, real-time modeling of watersheds, elevation, and terrain.

The Model ↑ top

How the Partnership Works

The ITLP is an Instructional Technology initiative that supports and promotes effective, creative uses of technology to enhance teaching and learning at Redlands. Each partnership is a collaboration between two parties:

Faculty Fellows

Faculty who sponsor a project they want to develop, in partnership with Instructional Technology, over the course of a term or academic year.

Instructional Designers

Each selected project is assigned an Instructional Designer from the Instructional Technology team, who works directly with the faculty fellow from initial design through implementation and assessment, and connects the project to the right technology resources.

What Projects the ITLP Supports

Develop VR/AR simulations, virtual field trips, or 3D visualizations that give students experiences not otherwise possible in a traditional classroom.

  • VR/AR simulations and virtual field trips for scenarios that are costly, distant, or unsafe to replicate in person
  • 3D visualization and spatial modeling for STEM, design, and humanities coursework
  • Safe rehearsal of high-risk or high-cost procedures through simulation before real-world application
  • Headset lending and short VR/AR literacy sessions so students can use the tools independently
  • Collaborative virtual environments that bring remote, hybrid, or multi-section classes into one shared space

Incorporate design thinking, prototyping, and physical or digital fabrication into coursework through Makerspace tools and space.

  • 3D printing and rapid prototyping for student projects, capstones, and design assignments
  • Laser cutting and digital fabrication tied directly to course deliverables
  • Electronics, circuitry, and physical computing (Arduino, Raspberry Pi) modules for applied coursework
  • Design-thinking workshops built into the curriculum, not offered as a one-off add-on
  • Materials and consumables support so cost isn't a barrier to hands-on assignments

Rebuild how a course is structured and delivered in Canvas so the platform actively supports learning, not just hosts files.

  • Interactive, accessibility-checked modules built to WCAG standards
  • Mastery paths and adaptive release sequenced to individual student progress
  • Kaltura video repository integration with embedded, gradable comprehension checks
  • Standardized rubrics and grading workflows applied consistently across multiple sections
  • Analytics and early-alert dashboards that flag at-risk students before it's too late to intervene

Design, implement, and evaluate innovative pedagogical approaches that use learning technology to engage students and improve outcomes — particularly well suited to large or high-enrollment courses.

  • Polling, live-response, and low-stakes formative check-ins built directly into class sessions
  • Gamified or scenario-based activities that raise participation in large lecture sections
  • Peer-review and collaborative workflows supported by structured digital tools
  • Pre/post assessment design to measure engagement gains, not just anecdotal impressions

Design a new course or redesign an existing course for a new modality (online, hybrid, HyFlex) or reimagine it around new technologies.

  • Full course builds for online, hybrid, or HyFlex delivery from the ground up
  • Modality conversion of an existing in-person course without losing instructional quality
  • Backward-design mapping from learning outcomes to activities, assessments, and technology
  • Pilot-term testing with built-in revision before full rollout

Develop interactive digital course materials or shareable media that departments can reuse across sections and future terms.

  • Rebuilding static lecture slides or PDFs into interactive, media-rich modules
  • Departmental content libraries that standardize materials across multiple sections
  • Short-form video, animation, or narrated content for asynchronous instruction
  • Version-controlled templates so materials stay current term over term

Investment ↑ top

Funding: Stipends & Equipment

$1,000 – $6,000Funding per project
Award amount scales to the depth, scope, and duration of the proposed work — whether that means outfitting an XR Lab simulation, stocking Makerspace materials, or rebuilding a course's architecture in Canvas. Requested budgets are itemized in the application form and reviewed as part of the evaluation process.

Faculty Stipend

Compensation for time invested in course redesign, content development, and collaboration with an assigned Instructional Designer outside of normal contracted duties.

Equipment & Materials

Technology and software licenses, XR headsets or peripherals, Makerspace materials and supplies, recording equipment, or other tools tied directly to the project's goals.

Production & Development Support

Course material development or third-party platform and licensing fees needed to execute the project.

$1,000 – $2,500

Smaller-scope projects — a single module redesign, a short video series, or a modest Makerspace activity.

$2,500 – $6,000

Larger, multi-course or multi-term projects involving significant XR development, custom equipment, or full course redesigns.

Requirements ↑ top

What's Required of Faculty Fellows

  • Applicants must be full-time University of Redlands faculty (including URLA campus faculty) and submit a description of the project they wish to sponsor.
  • The proposed project must be endorsed by the relevant department or school.
  • Faculty fellows meet periodically as a cohort — virtually or in person — to share progress and provide feedback to fellow participants.
  • Faculty fellows commit to participating in project assessment and, where appropriate, sharing outcomes with the broader campus community.

Selection ↑ top

Evaluation Criteria

Because resources are limited, only a select number of projects can be supported each cycle. Proposals are evaluated on:

  • Relevance to department, school, and institutional priorities
  • Anticipated impact on student learning
  • Feasibility of the proposed project plan and timeline
  • Clarity of how project success will be measured
  • Potential for the approach to be applied to other courses or programs
  • Reasonableness of the requested budget relative to project scope
